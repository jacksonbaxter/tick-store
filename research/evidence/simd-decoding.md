### P1. Lemire & Boytsov, "Decoding billions of integers per second through vectorization"
URL https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.2137 (arXiv v7, 30 Jan 2021). Cached: CACHE/downloads/lemire-boytsov-2012/paper.pdf, paper.txt.
Common fields for P1, from Section 6.1 and 6.2 (pp. 16-17): Intel Core i7 2600, 3.40 GHz, 8192 KB L3, 16 GB DDR3-1333 dual channel. GCC 4.7, -O3 (-mssse3 / -msse4.1 where needed). Threads: not stated. Speed is wall-clock in-memory processing, "Wall-clock times include the time necessary for differential coding and decoding." 32-bit integers. Memcpy on this box: 2270 mis (Section 6.1). Publication date of the measurements: not stated in the text (the arXiv version is 2021, the hardware and GCC are about 2012).

- P1.1 Vectorized prefix sum vs scalar: approximately 5000 mis vs approximately 2000 mis (mis = millions of integers per second). Scalar is 1.7 cycles/int. Quote: "Vectorized differential decoding is much faster (≈5000 mis vs. ≈2000 mis)." Location: Section 3, p. 10. ISA: SSE2. Data: not stated for this sentence. Counted: integers/s for the differential-decoding step. Note the vectorized form is delta over stride 4 (x_i - x_(i-4)), NOT a true prefix sum. Applicability: context (old x86), but directly relevant in design: it shows the trade taken.
- P1.2 Cost of stride-4 deltas: up to 2 bits per integer. Quote: "vectorized deltas are, on average, four times larger which increases the storage cost by up to 2 bits". Location: Section 3, p. 10. Typical: "SIMD-BP128? only uses about one extra bit per integer" (Section 6.4, p. 19). Data: Table IV shows 7.0 vs 6.0 bits/int on long ClusterData. Applicability: direct design evidence for the ratio given up for vectorizable delta; hardware independent, data is synthetic sorted integers, not ITCH timestamps.
- P1.3 Vectorized prefix sum "can be twice as fast"; prefix sum can be the majority of run time. Quote: "the prefix sum can account for the majority of the running time." Location: Section 1, p. 3. Also "a straightforward implementation of differential decoding can be four times slower than the decompression of small integers" (Section 3, p. 9). Applicability: context, supports targeting the prefix-sum step.
- P1.4 Table IV (synthetic, Section 6.4, p. 21). Long ClusterData arrays (single array of 2^25 ints, in [0, 2^29)), decode mis / bits per int: SIMD-BP128* 2800 / 7.0; SIMD-FastPFOR* 2400 / 6.8; SIMD-BP128 1900 / 6.0; SIMD-FastPFOR 1600 / 5.4; varint-G8IU 1300 / 9.0; Simple-8b 890 / 5.6; Variable Byte 830 / 8.1. (* = vectorized differential coding.) Quote: "Coding and decoding speed in millions of integers per second over synthetic data sets". ISA: SSE2 for SIMD-BP128 family, SSSE3 for varint-G8IU, others scalar (Table I and Section 6.2). Applicability: context (old x86, synthetic).
- P1.5 Bit unpacking alone: approximately 6000 mis for widths of 8 bits or less, block of at least 128 ints. Quote: "We can unpack integers having a bit width of 8 or less at a rate of ≈6000 mis." Location: Section 6.3, p. 18, Fig. 10b. Also "roughly twice as fast as the scalar version". Discussion: "they encode/decode integers at speeds of 4000–8500 mis" (Section 7, p. 26). Applicability: context.
- P1.6 Vertical vs horizontal layout: vertical "up to 70% faster" for widths under 8 or over 27, equal for 16-26. Location: Section 6.3, p. 18. Applicability: context; the design point (interleaved layout) carries over.
- P1.7 Table I, p. 3: SIMD-BP128* 2300 mis, 1.5 cycles/int, Core i7 3.4 GHz, SSE2 ("Results are not directly comparable"). Realistic data, Table I's own words: "on realistic data". Applicability: context.

### P2. Lemire, Boytsov, Kurz, "SIMD Compression and the Intersection of Sorted Integers"
URL https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.6399 (arXiv v13, 20 Apr 2020). Cached: CACHE/downloads/lemire-simd-intersect-2014/paper.pdf, paper.txt.
Common fields: Intel Xeon E5-1620 Sandy Bridge, 3.60 GHz, 10 MB L3, 32 kB L1d, 256 kB L2, 32 GB DDR3-1600 quad-channel (Section 7.2, p. 16). GCC 4.7, -O3 (Section 7.1). Threads: not stated. 32-bit ints.

- P2.1 Table III, p. 11: delta form vs size of deltas vs instructions/int on 4-lane SIMD: D1 1.0x, 1.5; D2 2.0x, 1; DM 2.5x, 0.5; D4 4.0x, 0.25. Quote: "Comparison between the 4 vectorized differential coding techniques with 4-integer SIMD instructions". ISA: SSE2 named in the text. Data: analytic estimate, not measured. Applicability: direct design evidence; this is the menu of delta shapes for tick-store's timestamp column.
- P2.2 Table V, p. 21 (ClusterData, 2^16 ints in [0, 2^19), "dense" / [0, 2^30), "sparse"; bits/int, billions of 32-bit ints/s). S4-BP128-D4 6.0, 5.4 / 16.5, 4.4. S4-BP128-DM 5.9, 5.5 / 16.3, 4.1. S4-BP128-D2 5.5, 4.8 / 16.0, 3.5. S4-BP128-D1 5.0, 3.9 / 15.5, 3.0. -NI (two-pass) D4 6.0, 3.9 / 16.5, 3.3. -NI D1 5.0, 3.0 / 15.5, 2.7. FAST PFOR scalar 4.4, 1.1 / 14.8, 1.1. VARINT 8.0, 1.2 / 17.2, 0.3. Memcpy 5.4. Entropy of deltas 3.9 / 14.7 bits. Quote: "We report decompression speed in billions of 32-bit integers per second (Bint32/s)." ISA: SSE (128-bit "S4" = 4 ints, Section 1 and Algorithm 1). Applicability: context (old x86, synthetic sorted ints), but the D1 to D4 ratio (5.0 to 6.0 bits, 3.9 to 5.4 Bint/s) is the cleanest published quantification of what stride deltas buy and cost.
- P2.3 Integrating prefix sum with unpacking: "For D4, integration improves the speed by anywhere from over 30 % to 90 %. For D1 ... the gains range from 20 % to 40 %." Location: Section 7.4, p. 19-20 (quote is split by a page break, joined here). Applicability: context; design guidance for a one-pass decoder.
- P2.4 Best case: "they decompress 32-bit integers using between 0.7 and 0.8 CPU cycles per integer." Section 7.5, p. 21. Theoretical two-pass limit 7.2 billion ints/s at 3.60 GHz; one-pass nearly 9 billion (Section 5, p. 11). Applicability: context.
- P2.5 Prefix sum share: "the prefix sum can account for up to half of the running time." Section 1, p. 2. Applicability: context.

### P3. Lemire, Kurz, Rupp, "Stream VByte: Faster Byte-Oriented Integer Compression"
URL https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.08990. Cached: CACHE/downloads/streamvbyte-2017/paper.pdf, paper.txt. Published in Information Processing Letters 130, 2018 (per the streamvbyte README).
Fields (Section 5, p. 7): Intel i7-4770 Haswell, 3.4 GHz, Turbo and SpeedStep off, 32 kB L1, 256 kB L2, 8 MB L3, 32 GB DDR3-1600 dual channel, GCC 4.8 -O3, "All tests are single-threaded." ISA: SSSE3 ("two x64 SIMD" instructions, Section 1). Data: ClueWeb09 posting lists (real). Counted: billions of 32-bit ints/s decoded to a 4096-int L1 buffer, including prefix sum ("All decoding times include differential coding").

- P3.1 4.0 Bis (highly compressible) to 1.1 Bis (less compressible). Quote: "the reported speed ranges from 4.0 Bis for highly compressible data to 1.1 Bis for less compressible data." p. 8. Applicability: context (old x86, posting lists).
- P3.2 Relative: "always at least ≈ 2.5× faster than the conventional VByte decoder and sometimes nearly 5× faster." p. 8. Cost: "use .5 to 2 more bits per integer than VByte in these tests". p. 8. Applicability: the separation of control and data streams is the key design lesson (below); ratio cost quantified.
- P3.3 Prefix sum over 2^L lanes: "L shifts and L additions". Paragraph "Differential Coding", p. 3. Applicability: design fact, any ISA.

### P5. Afroozeh & Boncz, "The FastLanes Compression Layout: Decoding >100 Billion Integers per Second with Scalar Code", PVLDB 16(9), 2023
URL https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/33289/33289.pdf (also vldb.org). Cached: CACHE/downloads/simd-fastlanes-2023/paper.pdf, paper.txt. Page numbers below are printed journal pages (PDF page 8 = p. 2139).
Common fields: Table 2, p. 2139: Ice Lake 8375C AVX512 3.5 GHz; Zen3 EPYC 7R13 AVX2 3.6 GHz; Zen4 Ryzen 7950X AVX512 4.5 GHz; Apple M1 NEON 3.2 GHz; Graviton2 Neoverse-N1 NEON 2.5 GHz; Graviton3 modified Neoverse-V1 NEON 2.6 GHz. Single vector decoded 30M times, "all data is L1 resident", cycles per value; turbo disabled "where present"; clang++ (Section 3.1). Threads: not stated. Data content irrelevant to the kernels: "column contents do not influence performance at all. Only the bit-width matters" (p. 2140).

- P5.1 Peak: 70 tuples per cycle, T=8, W=1 (Ice Lake AVX-512 box in Fig. 8). Quote: "FastLanes can decompress 70 tuples per cycle for 8-bits types". p. 2140. Applicability: context; 8-bit, not tick data, and the headline ">100 billion" is this case.
- P5.2 Auto-vectorisation parity. Quote: "clang++ can auto-vectorize our Scalar code, matching the performance of explicit SIMD intrinsics". p. 2140 (Q4). Applicability: direct design evidence; a single scalar code path could serve NEON and AVX2 without intrinsics, no dispatch code for that kernel. Clang flags per ISA remain necessary.
- P5.3 SIMD beats Scalar: "40x-70x for 8-bits, to 3x-4x for 64-bits types." p. 2139. Applicability: context; 64-bit figure is the relevant one for timestamps.
- P5.4 SVE vs NEON on Graviton3. Quote: "our experiments on Graviton3 showed that SVE [30] is slower than NEON." p. 2139. Applicability: context, no direct use on M4 (no SVE in normal mode, see ISA section).
- P5.5 Layout comparison, Fig. 13, p. 2141: "the interleaved layout becomes respectively 2x and 4x faster on AVX2 and AVX512", while the 4-way layout gains little. T=32, Ice Lake. Applicability: context; Zen 1 execution units are 128-bit (I3), so a 2x AVX2 gain must not be assumed there.
- P5.6 DELTA alternatives, Table 3, p. 2142. Decompression cost in ADDs for S values / compression overhead bits: Scalar S / 0; Four Cursor S / small; Vertical 2 / 0; Horizontal log S / 0; Tree 2 / 0; D4 1 / log S; DM 2 / a log term; Unified Transposed Layout 1 / 1024/S. Quote: "Compression Overhead is the number of extra bits required." Fig. 15 caption: "DELTA decoding on the Unified Transposed layout is 3x-40x faster than the alternatives (Ice Lake)." Applicability: direct design evidence for tick-store timestamps. Ice Lake only for Fig. 15.
- P5.7 Overhead of the transposed layout with delta-coded bases. Quote: "(7+log(64)+1)/64)=0.21 bit per value. So that turns W=7 bits per value into 7.21 bits per value (3% overhead)." Section 4, p. 2143. Applicability: direct design evidence, T=64 case. It is arithmetic in the paper, not measured on data. Without delta-coding bases: 1 bit per value (p. 2142). Layout: fixed vectors of 1024, one bit width per vector, bases per lane.
- P5.8 Figure 10 reading for M1 vs Scalar. Quote: "In terms of scalar performance, M1 tops Ice Lake clock-for-clock." p. 2140. And "M1 also has just 128-bit NEON, but clearly has more instruction level paralellism (ILP)." Applicability: context (M1, not M4).

### P7. Blalock, Madden, Guttag, "Sprintz: Time Series Compression for the Internet of Things", PACM IMWUT 2(3), Article 93, Sept 2018
URL https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.02515. Cached: CACHE/downloads/sprintz-2018/paper.pdf, paper.txt.
Common fields: "a single thread on a 2013 Macbook Pro with a 2.6GHz Intel Core i7-4960HQ processor" (Section 5, p. 93:12); memory not stated; 256-bit SIMD ("32B vector registers", Fig. 4 caption); best of ten runs. Data widths: 8 and 16 bit only.

- P7.1 Speed: "can decompress at up to 3GB/s in a single thread." Section 1. Without Huffman "multiple GB/s once rows exceed ∼16B"; with Huffman "consistently decompresses at over 500MB/s". Section 5.4, p. 93:15. Data: synthetic, 100M random values, 1 to 80 columns. Applicability: context; multi-column 8/16-bit, not 64-bit single-column ticks.
- P7.2 Format trade-off: blocks of 8 samples, per-block per-column bit width header, zero-block run lengths, bit packing "with extremely small block sizes". Section 4. Quote: "up to seven bits of padding are added at the end of each row so that all rows begin on a byte boundary" and for univariate 8-bit data row padding "makes compression ratios greater than 1 impossible." Section 4.2, p. 93:10. Applicability: direct design evidence: this is exactly fixed-width-per-block plus byte alignment in exchange for SIMD.
- P7.3 Serial limit stays even with SIMD. Quote: "The limiting factor is serial dependence between decoding one sample and predicting the next one". p. 93:16. "this is accelerated by having wider samples that fill a vector register, but not by having longer blocks." Applicability: direct: predictor-in-the-loop decoding does not vectorize along time. Single-column timestamps get the slow case.
- P7.4 Predictors in isolation, vectorized: Fire decodes "up to 6GB/s", "nearly the same speed as the competing methods" (delta and double delta), memcpy 7.5 GB/s. Section 5.6, p. 93:16, Fig. 6. Applicability: context; one of the few places delta and double delta are compared side by side for speed. Fig. 6 numbers for double delta alone are in a plot only, not transcribed.
- P7.5 x86 note, "bit packing and unpacking can be accelerated with the pext and pdep instructions" p. 93:11. Applicability: do not reuse on Zen 1 (I3).

### P8. Loncaric, Jeppesen, Zinberg, "Pcodec: Better Compression for Numerical Sequences", arXiv 2502.06112v2, 25 May 2025 (preprint; peer-review status not stated in the text)
URL https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06112. Cached: CACHE/downloads/pcodec-2025/paper.pdf, paper.txt.
Common fields: "bare-metal 96-core Intel Xeon Platinum 8488C CPU" (Section 3.2, p. 7). Clock, memory, ISA of SIMD paths: not stated. Thread counts 1 and 48. Datasets limited to 2M rows. Median of at least 3 iterations.

- P8.1 Table 3, p. 8, single-thread decompression MiB/s, Pco: Air Quality 2,206; Taxi 2,298; r/place 2,493; Housing 1,432; Payments 3,980; Twitter 5,450. Parquet Delta+Zstd for comparison 2,272 / 2,136 / 2,310 / 1,679 / 3,728 / 4,910. Turbo PFor+Zstd 2,902 / 1,334 / 2,101 / 1,015 / 3,345 / 4,877. - P8.2 Structure that makes it vectorizable. Quote: "we use 4-way interleaved tANS to accelerate entropy coding and decoding." and "we structured each batch of latents as 256 tANS codes followed by 256 offsets, so by the time we reach the offsets, we have full information about which bit range each one occupies." ### I3. Zen 1 instruction costs (Agner Fog, Instruction tables, last updated 2025-09-20)
URL https://www.agner.org/optimize/instruction_tables.pdf. Cached: CACHE/downloads/simd-agner/instruction_tables.pdf, t.txt. Section "AMD Zen 1" measured on Ryzen 7 1800X. Columns are Ops / latency / reciprocal throughput. These are the numbers to plan the Linux host with, subject to the host actually being a Zen 1 family part.
- PEXT r,r,r: 7 / 18 / 18 (PDF p. 90). PDEP r,r,r: 6 / 18 / 18 (p. 90). Zen 3 for comparison: PEXT and PDEP 1 / 3 / 1 (p. 116).
- VPSRLVD/Q y,y,y: 2 / 3 / 4 (p. 96); the x,x,x form is 1 / 3 / 2. So variable-per-lane shift on 256-bit is 4 cycles reciprocal throughput on Zen 1.
- VPGATHERQQ y: 35 ops, reciprocal throughput 12 (p. 94). VPGATHERDD y: 66 ops, 20.
- VPSHUFB y: 2 / 1 / 1 (p. 93); PSHUFB x: 1 / 1 / 0.5. VPMOVMSKB r32,y: 2 / 3 / 2 (p. 94). TZCNT 2 / 2 / 0.5; LZCNT 1 / 1 / 0.25; BZHI, SHRX, BEXTR 1 op, latency 1, reciprocal 0.25 (p. 90).
- Agner Fog, The microarchitecture of Intel, AMD and VIA CPUs, chapter 22 (AMD Zen), PDF p. 235, https://www.agner.org/optimize/microarchitecture.pdf, cached dh-cpu/agner-uarch.pdf. Quote: "The execution units have 128 bit width on Zen 1 and 256 bits in Zen 2." and "typical 256-bit vector instruction generates two µops on Zen 1, but only one µop on Zen 2." and "There is one store unit of 128 bits on Zen 1". Same page, Section 22.11: "256-bit AVX and AVX2 instructions are split into two µops that do 128 bits each."
Applicability: direct design evidence for the x86 path. Avoid PEXT/PDEP and gather on Zen 1. Scalar BZHI/SHRX/LZCNT are cheap. AVX2 on Zen 1 gives no doubling of store throughput over 128-bit, so FastLanes-style store-bound kernels may gain little from 256-bit there (inference, must be measured).

## Synthesis: what the sources say about the core question
Everything here cites entries above; lines marked (mine) are inference.

1. A sequential variable-length bit stream resists SIMD for two separate reasons. Bit position of value i depends on the width of value i-1 (control dependency), and the value depends on the previous value (data dependency). ALP says Gorilla's branches and dependencies "prevent SIMD" (P6.2). TsFile profile puts 81.8% of samples in control-bit parsing and variable-width reads (B-2). The data dependency alone is the cheap part: XOR inverse on plain words reaches 10.8 to 21.5 GB/s on Neoverse V2 (B-7).
2. Nobody found a SIMD decoder for the unmodified Gorilla bit stream. The two working routes are (a) a better scalar decoder with the format unchanged, 1.76x to 2.57x (B-2), and (b) a format change.
3. Every format that vectorizes does the same thing in some mix: fix the width per block, move control data out of line, split into independent lanes.

| Design | What it gives up | Decode gain | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| D4 instead of D1 deltas | up to 2 bits/int; 1.0 bit on Table V dense (5.0 to 6.0) | 3.9 to 5.4 Bint/s (1.4x) on SSE, Xeon E5-1620 | P2.1, P2.2 |
| FastLanes unified transposed layout | 1 bit/value, or 0.21 bit with delta-coded bases, T=64, W=7 (3%, arithmetic); fixed 1024 vector; one width per vector | 3x-40x over alternatives, Ice Lake; M1 rsum T=64 0.551 vs 0.910 cycles/value | P5.6, P5.7, table |
| Parquet DELTA_BINARY_PACKED / TS_2DIFF | min delta per block, width per miniblock, multiple of 32 values; loses DoD's 1-bit case (mine) | Zen 2 AVX2: 30-60% from better unpack | B0.1, Parquet Encodings.md |
| Sprintz | 8-sample blocks, per-column width header, padding, 8/16-bit only | up to 3 GB/s; serial limit remains | P7 |
| Pcodec | batches of 256, tANS codes before offsets, 4-way interleave | over 1 GiB/s per thread | P8 |
| Interleaved ANS | no metadata for ANS; a format change | 2-way 1.6x+, 8-way SSE 2.15x-2.79x | P9 |
| Middle-out XOR | 8 segments, per-block length, byte-rounded offsets; ratio 1.3-3.3 | scalar 2.3-2.9, AVX-512 3.4-4.8 GB/s, Skylake-X 2.0 GHz | B-3 |

4. Delta vs delta-of-delta. Stride tricks (D2, D4) apply to one accumulation; delta-of-delta needs two (B-7 reverse recurrence out[i] = in[i] + 2*out[i-1] - out[i-2]). Reconstruct-only cost is 2.4x to 3.0x relative to delta on Neoverse V2 (B-7, mine). Parquet and TS_2DIFF subtract a per-block min delta instead: code is a single prefix sum, first-order ("int32_t val = (int32_t)delta + prev + delta_min_", ts2diff_decoder.h, read_opt branch, WebFetch summary), though IoTDB's paper calls TS_2DIFF "a variant of delta-of-delta" (cached iotdb-encoding/p2148-song.pdf, Section 4.1.1, PDF p. 3). Parquet spec text: "Compute the frame of reference (the minimum of the deltas in the block). Subtract this min delta from all deltas in the block." (cached parquet-encodings/Encodings.md lines 243-247). Applicability: this is the natural SIMD-friendly stand-in for delta-of-delta on nearly regular timestamps (mine); ratio loss vs true DoD on ITCH timestamps must be measured, no source gives it.
5. Prefix-sum lanes. On M4 the classic 4-lane shift-add was slower than scalar, and 16-value interleaved load gave 2.3x, 32-bit (B1). On Neoverse V2 FastPFoR's path was slower than scalar and a shortened carry chain gave 1.8x (B-7). Arm's own PR says 64-bit scalar beats NEON (B-5). FastLanes' M1 data shows NEON at T=64 is 1.65x on transposed data (P5 table, mine). 64-bit is the weakest case on every source that reports it.
6. Fused kernels repeatedly beat two passes: P2.3 (30-90%), P6.6 (40%), B-9, FastLanes Fig. 11.
7. Per-architecture plan implied by sources (mine, not a measurement). arm64: NEON baseline, no runtime dispatch (simdjson), no SME/SVE (I1, I2), no pext (I4a), auto-vectorized scalar kernels are competitive (P5.2). x86-64 on Zen 1: AVX2 present, but avoid PEXT/PDEP (18 cycles) and gather; `bmi2` CPUID alone is not a safe test (I4); variable shift y,y,y costs reciprocal 4 and x,x,x costs 2 (I3), so 256-bit gives no gain in bytes per cycle for that instruction. FastLanes-style scalar code that uses only shifts, ANDs and ORs avoids all of these (P5.2).
